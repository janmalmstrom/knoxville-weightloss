How much does Zepbound cost without insurance?

Quick Answer

Zepbound costs $499–$1,086 per month without insurance depending on dose; Eli Lilly's savings card can reduce this to $550/month or less for eligible commercially insured patients.

Key Takeaways

  • Zepbound's list price ranges from approximately $938–$1,086/month at full retail; Eli Lilly's direct savings program offers vials at $349–$499/month for self-pay patients.
  • Zepbound (tirzepatide) is FDA-approved for chronic weight management as of November 2023; it is not the same as Mounjaro, which is tirzepatide approved for type 2 diabetes.
  • The SURMOUNT-1 trial showed an average 22.5% body weight reduction with tirzepatide 15mg over 72 weeks — the strongest weight loss efficacy data of any currently available GLP-1 medication.
  • Tennessee's roughly 36% adult obesity rate is among the highest in the US, and many East Tennessee patients pay out-of-pocket because most commercial and employer plans still exclude weight loss medications from coverage.

What Is Zepbound, and Why Does the Price Vary So Much?

Zepbound is tirzepatide — the same active molecule as Mounjaro — but specifically FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or a BMI of 27 or higher with at least one weight-related condition such as hypertension, high cholesterol, or obstructive sleep apnea. The FDA granted that approval in November 2023, making Zepbound only the second GLP-1 medication (after Wegovy) approved specifically for obesity rather than diabetes.

Tirzepatide works through a dual mechanism: it activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors simultaneously. That dual action is reflected in the SURMOUNT-1 trial — published in the New England Journal of Medicine by Jastreboff et al. in 2022 — which recorded an average body weight loss of 22.5% over 72 weeks at the 15mg dose. That figure exceeds the efficacy seen with semaglutide in the STEP 1 trial (14.9% average over 68 weeks, Wilding et al., NEJM 2021), though both represent substantially greater weight loss than any previously available non-surgical treatment.

Price variation in Zepbound comes from three factors: the dose prescribed, the pharmacy dispensing it, and whether any manufacturer or third-party savings program applies. Zepbound is available in six doses — 2.5mg, 5mg, 7.5mg, 10mg, 12.5mg, and 15mg — and higher doses carry higher list prices. Most patients begin at 2.5mg and titrate upward over several months, so cost is not static across a treatment course.

💡 Clinical note: Zepbound and Mounjaro contain identical active ingredients (tirzepatide), but they carry different FDA indications and are listed separately on insurance formularies. A patient whose insurer covers Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes may find Zepbound for obesity is excluded — or vice versa. A licensed provider can help clarify which indication applies to your situation.

Eli Lilly set Zepbound's monthly list price at approximately $1,059–$1,086 per four-week supply at launch (all doses) — modestly lower than Wegovy's list price of approximately $1,349/month. List price is rarely what a self-pay patient actually pays. Here is a breakdown of the realistic cost tiers:

Does Insurance Cover Zepbound in Tennessee?

Coverage for Zepbound varies sharply by insurance type — and East Tennessee patients often face coverage gaps that residents of other states do not.

Commercial (Employer or Marketplace) Insurance

No federal mandate requires commercial health plans to cover FDA-approved obesity medications. Under ACA rules, marketplace plans must cover preventive services but are not required to cover weight loss medications. As a result, many employer-sponsored plans in Tennessee — including plans common across Knox County's large employer base spanning UT Medical Center, Oak Ridge National Laboratory contractors, and the Covenant Health system workforce — explicitly exclude weight loss drugs from formulary. Some larger self-insured employers have begun adding GLP-1 coverage, but exclusions remain the norm in 2024–2025.

If your commercial plan covers Zepbound, prior authorization is typically required. Criteria generally include documented BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with comorbidity), evidence of prior weight loss attempts, and a prescription written specifically for the weight management indication.

Medicare

Medicare Part D does not cover weight loss medications, including Zepbound, when prescribed solely for obesity. This is a statutory exclusion — Part D plans are prohibited from covering drugs "used for weight loss" under the Medicare Modernization Act, a restriction CMS has noted explicitly (cms.gov). The exception: a Medicare patient with type 2 diabetes prescribed Mounjaro for that indication may have Part D coverage — but that is a diabetes indication, not Zepbound for weight loss. The Treat and Reduce Obesity Act, which would change this, has not yet been enacted. Tennessee's Medicare-age population, large relative to national averages, is currently excluded from coverage in most cases.

TennCare (Tennessee Medicaid)

TennCare does not cover Zepbound for weight loss. Tennessee's Medicaid program does not include GLP-1 medications for obesity as a standard benefit, though coverage of tirzepatide under a diabetes indication (Mounjaro) may be available for qualifying enrollees with type 2 diabetes.

What Does a Full Zepbound Treatment Course Cost in Knoxville?

Monthly cost is only part of the equation. Zepbound is not a short-term medication — SURMOUNT-1 ran 72 weeks, and clinical evidence indicates that weight regain commonly occurs when GLP-1 therapy is discontinued. Obesity medicine guidelines now treat GLP-1 receptor agonists as long-term therapy for a chronic condition, analogous to antihypertensives for blood pressure management.

A realistic cost projection for a Knoxville patient using the Lilly Direct self-pay vial program:

  • Months 1–2 (2.5mg dose): $349/month = $698
  • Months 3–4 (5mg dose): $349/month = $698
  • Months 5+ (7.5mg–15mg, as titrated): $499/month ongoing
  • Year 1 estimated total (self-pay, Lilly Direct): approximately $4,894–$5,592

These figures exclude physician visit fees, baseline lab work (metabolic panel, thyroid function, HbA1c), and monitoring appointments — all medically necessary for safe GLP-1 prescribing. How to Minimize Zepbound Cost Without Insurance

Knoxville patients managing Zepbound costs out of pocket have several options that do not require compromising on the FDA-approved product:

  • Lilly Direct self-pay vial program: $349–$499/month directly from Eli Lilly with a valid prescription written for the vial formulation. Available at LillyDirect.com.
  • Lilly savings card: If your commercial insurance does not cover Zepbound, the savings card may cap cost at approximately $550/month. Confirm current eligibility at LillyDirect.com — terms change periodically.
  • Prior authorization appeal: A licensed provider can assist with a prior authorization request documenting BMI, comorbidities, and prior treatment history. Strong clinical documentation improves approval rates.
  • HSA/FSA funds: Zepbound purchased for its FDA-approved chronic weight management indication is generally eligible for HSA and FSA reimbursement. Confirm with your plan administrator.
  • Physician-supervised titration: Starting at 2.5mg and advancing only as clinically tolerated keeps patients at the lowest effective dose longer — directly reducing monthly cost during the titration phase.

The SURMOUNT-1 trial demonstrated 22.5% average body weight loss at the 15mg dose over 72 weeks, the strongest efficacy data available for any non-surgical weight loss medication. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest way to get Zepbound without insurance?

The lowest-cost FDA-approved option is Eli Lilly's direct self-pay vial program at $349/month for the 2.5mg or 5mg dose and $499/month for higher doses, purchased through LillyDirect.com with a valid prescription. Compounded tirzepatide has been cheaper, but the FDA has restricted compounding as the Zepbound shortage resolved in late 2024, and compounded versions are not FDA-approved. GoodRx discounts typically do not beat the Lilly direct price for self-pay patients.

Is Zepbound the same as Mounjaro?

Both contain tirzepatide, but they carry different FDA indications. Mounjaro is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes (May 2022); Zepbound is FDA-approved for chronic weight management (November 2023). Saying Mounjaro is approved for weight loss is inaccurate — weight loss is an established off-label use of Mounjaro, while Zepbound is the on-label weight management product. This distinction matters for insurance coverage, as each drug is evaluated separately on formularies.

Does Medicare cover Zepbound for weight loss in Tennessee?

No. Medicare Part D is statutorily prohibited from covering medications prescribed solely for weight loss, including Zepbound. CMS has confirmed this exclusion explicitly. The only pathway for Medicare patients to access tirzepatide with Part D coverage is if they have type 2 diabetes and receive a prescription for Mounjaro under that indication — not for weight management. Proposed legislation to change this (the Treat and Reduce Obesity Act) has not been enacted.

Does TennCare cover Zepbound?

TennCare (Tennessee Medicaid) does not currently cover Zepbound for weight loss. Tennessee's Medicaid program does not list GLP-1 medications for obesity as a standard benefit. Patients with type 2 diabetes enrolled in TennCare may have access to Mounjaro for the diabetes indication, depending on formulary — but that is a diabetes treatment, not a weight loss program.

How effective is Zepbound compared to Wegovy?

Head-to-head trial data are limited, but comparing the SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide) and STEP-1 (semaglutide) trials, tirzepatide produced an average 22.5% body weight loss at the 15mg dose over 72 weeks versus 14.9% for semaglutide 2.4mg over 68 weeks. These are different trials with different populations, so direct comparison has limitations, but tirzepatide's dual GLP-1/GIP mechanism appears to produce meaningfully greater weight loss. A licensed provider can help determine which medication is more appropriate based on individual health history.

Can I use my HSA or FSA to pay for Zepbound?

Yes, generally. Zepbound prescribed for its FDA-approved indication — chronic weight management — is typically an eligible expense for Health Savings Accounts (HSA) and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA). This can meaningfully reduce the effective after-tax cost for patients paying out of pocket, since HSA and FSA contributions are made with pre-tax dollars. Confirm eligibility with your specific plan administrator before purchasing.

What does physician-supervised Zepbound treatment include beyond the medication cost?

Safe GLP-1 prescribing requires baseline labs (metabolic panel, thyroid function, HbA1c), an assessment of contraindications — including personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2 — and ongoing dose titration with side effect monitoring. Monthly monitoring visits allow the provider to adjust the dose schedule and manage gastrointestinal side effects, which occur in a majority of patients during titration. Why do so many Knoxville patients pay for Zepbound out of pocket?

Tennessee has one of the highest obesity rates in the US — approximately 36% of adults — yet neither Medicare nor TennCare covers GLP-1 medications for weight loss, and commercial plan coverage is inconsistent across the Knox County employer base. The large workforce in sectors like healthcare (UT Medical Center, Covenant Health) and federal contracting (Oak Ridge National Laboratory area) is often covered by self-insured employer plans that have been slow to add obesity medication benefits. This coverage gap means a significant portion of medically eligible East Tennessee patients fund treatment out of pocket, making programs like Lilly's direct vial pricing and HSA utilization practically important.
