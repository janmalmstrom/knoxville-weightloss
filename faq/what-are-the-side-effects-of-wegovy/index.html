Safety & Side Effects

What are the side effects of Wegovy?

By Ashley Morgan

Quick Answer

Wegovy's most common side effects are gastrointestinal — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation — occurring most often during dose escalation and typically improving over time.

Key Takeaways

  • In STEP 1 clinical trial data, nausea affected approximately 44% of semaglutide 2.4mg participants, but fewer than 5% discontinued treatment due to GI side effects.
  • Wegovy carries an FDA boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors based on rodent studies; it is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN2 syndrome.
  • The slow dose-escalation protocol — starting at 0.25mg weekly and titrating over 16–20 weeks to the 2.4mg maintenance dose — exists specifically to reduce GI side effect severity.
  • Rare but serious risks include acute pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, and hypoglycemia (primarily in patients also taking insulin or sulfonylureas); physician monitoring is required to detect these early.

What Does the FDA Say About Wegovy's Safety Profile?

Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg, subcutaneous injection) received FDA approval for chronic weight management in June 2021 — the first approval of its kind in nearly a decade. That approval came with a detailed safety label distinguishing between common side effects, which are manageable and expected, and rare serious risks, which require physician monitoring.

FDA prescribing information for Wegovy identifies gastrointestinal events as the most frequently reported adverse effects across the STEP clinical trial program. These trials enrolled over 4,500 participants across multiple studies, providing one of the largest safety datasets for any weight loss medication to date. What that data actually shows — rather than what circulates in anecdotal reports — is what Knoxville patients need before deciding whether physician-supervised GLP-1 therapy is appropriate for their situation.

⚠️ Important: Wegovy carries an FDA boxed warning — the agency's most serious warning category — for the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors. This risk was observed in rodent studies at clinically relevant exposures. Wegovy is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2). The licensed Knoxville providers we connect you with will screen for these contraindications before prescribing.

The Most Common Side Effects: Gastrointestinal Events

In STEP 1 (Wilding et al., 2021, New England Journal of Medicine) — the landmark trial establishing semaglutide 2.4mg's efficacy with a mean 14.9% body weight reduction over 68 weeks — gastrointestinal side effects were the most frequently reported adverse events in the semaglutide group:

  • Nausea: ~44% of participants (vs. ~16% placebo)
  • Diarrhea: ~30% of participants (vs. ~16% placebo)
  • Vomiting: ~24% of participants (vs. ~6% placebo)
  • Constipation: ~24% of participants (vs. ~11% placebo)
  • Abdominal pain: ~20% of participants (vs. ~13% placebo)

Context matters here. The vast majority of GI events were rated mild to moderate in severity, and fewer than 5% of semaglutide participants in STEP 1 discontinued treatment due to GI side effects — meaning most patients moved through the discomfort and continued therapy.

Timing also matters. GI symptoms are most pronounced during dose escalation, when the body is adjusting to each new dose level, and typically diminish once a stable maintenance dose is reached. Nausea, in particular, tends to peak in the first few weeks at a new dose before settling.

💡 Clinical note: Wegovy's dose escalation schedule — 0.25mg for 4 weeks, then 0.5mg, 1.0mg, 1.7mg, and finally 2.4mg maintenance, each for 4 weeks — was specifically designed to reduce GI side effect severity. Rushing this schedule increases side effect burden without improving efficacy. Providers who advance patients to maintenance dose ahead of schedule are not following the protocol that produced the STEP trial results.

Serious but Rare Risks: What Physician Monitoring Is Designed to Catch

Physician supervision with GLP-1 therapy matters because of what it catches — not merely as a formality. Several serious adverse effects are documented in Wegovy's FDA label, rare in absolute frequency but clinically significant when they occur.

Acute Pancreatitis

GLP-1 receptor agonists have been associated with acute pancreatitis in post-marketing surveillance and some clinical data, though a definitive causal relationship has not been established. Wegovy's label advises discontinuing the medication if pancreatitis is confirmed. Patients should report persistent, severe abdominal pain — particularly pain radiating to the back — promptly. Baseline and follow-up lab work ordered by licensed providers is one mechanism for detecting early signals.

Gallbladder Disease

Rapid weight loss of any kind increases gallstone risk, and semaglutide is no exception. Cholelithiasis (gallstones) and cholecystitis (gallbladder inflammation) occurred at higher rates in STEP trial participants on semaglutide than on placebo. Abdominal ultrasound may be warranted if a patient develops right upper quadrant pain during treatment.

Hypoglycemia

In patients without type 2 diabetes — which describes many Wegovy candidates — hypoglycemia risk is low. The risk increases substantially when Wegovy is combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues such as sulfonylureas. Patients with type 2 diabetes who are prescribed Wegovy require careful coordination of their diabetes medications as glucose control improves on GLP-1 therapy. This is a clinical scenario where physician oversight is not optional.

Heart Rate Increase

A mean increase of approximately 1–4 beats per minute in resting heart rate has been documented with semaglutide. This is generally clinically insignificant for most patients but warrants monitoring in those with pre-existing cardiac arrhythmias.

Suicidal Ideation

The FDA issued a safety communication in 2023 investigating reports of suicidal ideation associated with GLP-1 medications, including semaglutide. Subsequent analysis — including data from the SELECT cardiovascular outcomes trial — did not establish a causal link, and the European Medicines Agency reached a similar conclusion. Wegovy's label includes monitoring guidance, and this remains an area of active pharmacovigilance.

Side Effects Specific to the Injection Site

Wegovy is administered as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection using a prefilled auto-injector pen. Injection-site reactions — redness, bruising, itching, or mild swelling — are reported by a subset of patients and are typically self-limiting. Rotating injection sites (abdomen, thigh, upper arm) reduces local irritation. Serious allergic reactions are rare but require immediate medical attention if they occur.

Wegovy Side Effects: Clinical Summary

  • Most common (GI): Nausea (~44%), diarrhea (~30%), vomiting (~24%), constipation (~24%) — typically mild-to-moderate, peak during dose escalation
  • Boxed warning: Thyroid C-cell tumors — contraindicated in personal or family history of MTC or MEN2
  • Rare serious risks: Acute pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, hypoglycemia (especially with insulin or sulfonylureas), modest heart rate increase
  • Injection site: Local reactions common, managed by rotating sites
  • Discontinuation due to side effects: Fewer than 5% in STEP 1 — most patients tolerate the medication when dose escalation is managed properly
  • Physician monitoring: Baseline labs, thyroid history screening, and follow-up appointments are standard protocol — not optional add-ons

Why Knoxville Patients Should Have This Conversation With a Physician — Not the Internet

Tennessee's obesity prevalence sits at approximately 36% — among the highest in the United States — and demand for GLP-1 medications across Knoxville and East Tennessee has grown substantially. With that growth has come an increase in options: telehealth services, compounded semaglutide providers, and medical spas offering weight loss injections with varying levels of clinical oversight.

Wegovy's side effect profile is manageable for most patients — but managing it requires someone who can distinguish nausea from pancreatitis, review current medications for interaction risks, and adjust the dose schedule when needed. Compounded semaglutide products are not FDA-approved; they were permitted under drug shortage provisions, and the FDA has been restricting compounding as brand shortages resolve. A patient who develops a side effect on a compounded product has fewer regulatory protections than one on FDA-approved Wegovy.

Patients already managing type 2 diabetes alongside weight loss goals face an additional layer of complexity. As GLP-1 therapy takes effect and glucose control improves, existing diabetes medications may require adjustment — a clinical process that requires coordinated physician care, not a standalone telehealth prescription.

Bottom Line

Wegovy's side effects are real but manageable for most patients. GI symptoms — primarily nausea — are the most common adverse events, affecting roughly 44% of participants in STEP 1, but fewer than 5% stopped treatment because of them. Serious risks including pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, and thyroid tumors are rare and are identified through screening and monitoring — which is precisely why physician supervision exists. Wegovy is FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults with BMI ≥30, or BMI ≥27 with at least one weight-related comorbidity. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common side effect of Wegovy?

Nausea is the most frequently reported side effect, occurring in approximately 44% of participants in the STEP 1 trial (Wilding et al., 2021, NEJM). It is most pronounced during the dose-escalation phase — when patients are moving up to a new dose level — and typically improves significantly once the 2.4mg maintenance dose is stabilized.

How long does nausea from Wegovy last?

Nausea typically peaks in the first few weeks after each dose increase and then diminishes. For most patients, it resolves substantially by the time they reach the maintenance dose at approximately week 20. Eating smaller, lower-fat meals and avoiding eating immediately before or after the injection can reduce nausea severity.

Is Wegovy safe for the heart?

Cardiovascular safety data for semaglutide is favorable. The SELECT trial — a large cardiovascular outcomes trial — found that semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke, cardiovascular death) by 20% in overweight and obese adults without diabetes. A mild increase in resting heart rate (1–4 bpm) is documented but clinically insignificant for most patients.

Who cannot take Wegovy?

Wegovy is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2 (MEN2) syndrome, due to the boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors observed in animal studies. It is also not indicated during pregnancy. Patients with a prior history of pancreatitis should discuss risk with their physician before starting.

Does Wegovy cause hair loss?

Hair loss (telogen effluvium) has been reported by some patients on Wegovy and other GLP-1 medications. This is believed to be related to rapid caloric restriction and significant weight loss rather than a direct pharmacological effect of semaglutide itself — the same pattern can occur with any intervention that produces rapid weight reduction. It is generally temporary.

Can Wegovy cause low blood sugar (hypoglycemia)?

In patients without type 2 diabetes, Wegovy carries a low risk of hypoglycemia on its own. The risk increases meaningfully when Wegovy is combined with insulin or sulfonylurea medications in patients who also have type 2 diabetes. In those cases, a physician may need to reduce insulin or sulfonylurea doses as semaglutide takes effect and glucose control improves.

What's the difference between Wegovy and Ozempic side effects?

Both contain semaglutide and share the same side effect class, but Wegovy (2.4mg) is a higher dose than Ozempic (0.5–2mg), which means GI side effects may be somewhat more pronounced at the Wegovy maintenance dose. Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; weight loss is an established off-label use. Wegovy is FDA-approved specifically for chronic weight management. The prescribing context and dose titration differ between the two.

Should I be worried about the thyroid cancer warning on Wegovy?

The boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors is based on rodent studies — C-cell tumors were observed in rats and mice at clinically relevant exposures. To date, the causal relationship in humans has not been established, and population-level epidemiological data has not shown a clear signal. However, the FDA requires the warning, and Wegovy remains contraindicated in anyone with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2. A licensed Knoxville provider will take a thyroid history before prescribing.
