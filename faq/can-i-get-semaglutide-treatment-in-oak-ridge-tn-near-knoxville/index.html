What semaglutide medications are available — and what is FDA-approved?

This distinction matters, and providers supervising GLP-1 therapy are obligated to clarify it. Two semaglutide-based products are available:

  • Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg subcutaneous injection): FDA-approved in June 2021 specifically for chronic weight management in adults with a BMI ≥30, or BMI ≥27 with at least one weight-related comorbidity such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes, or high cholesterol. This is the on-label choice for weight loss.
  • Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg subcutaneous injection): FDA-approved in December 2017 for glycemic control in type 2 diabetes. Weight loss with Ozempic is an established, widely used off-label application — but it is not FDA-approved for that indication. Providers can prescribe it off-label; patients should understand this distinction.

Compounded semaglutide — versions produced by compounding pharmacies rather than the branded manufacturer — is not FDA-approved. It was permitted under drug shortage provisions while Wegovy supply was constrained. As FDA supply resolutions progressed, compounding restrictions tightened. Oak Ridge patients considering compounded semaglutide should discuss current regulatory status directly with a licensed provider before proceeding.

⚠️ Important: Semaglutide in any form is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2). A physician evaluation — not an online intake form alone — is required to screen for these and other contraindications before any prescription is written.

What does the clinical evidence show for semaglutide?

The STEP trial program (Semaglutide Treatment Effect in People with Obesity) provides the core evidence base for semaglutide at the 2.4mg weight management dose. STEP 1 (Wilding et al., 2021, New England Journal of Medicine) enrolled 1,961 adults with obesity or overweight plus a weight-related comorbidity and no type 2 diabetes. Participants receiving semaglutide 2.4mg lost an average of 14.9% of body weight over 68 weeks, compared to 2.4% in the placebo group.

For context: a 250-pound Oak Ridge resident would expect an average loss of roughly 37 pounds over 68 weeks under STEP 1 protocol conditions — with physician supervision, lifestyle support, and consistent medication adherence. These are average figures; individual results vary, and not all participants achieve the trial average.

The SELECT trial extended the evidence: in adults who were overweight or obese without diabetes, semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% compared to placebo. For East Tennessee patients managing cardiovascular risk factors alongside obesity, this is clinically significant — semaglutide effects extend beyond weight loss alone.

How does physician-supervised treatment differ from telehealth-only prescribing?

Oak Ridge patients researching GLP-1 therapy will encounter multiple options: local clinics, Knoxville-area providers, and national telehealth platforms. The difference extends beyond setting to the depth of oversight.

Physician-supervised programs connected through Knoxville Weight Loss Clinic typically include:

  • Baseline laboratory evaluation: Thyroid panel, complete metabolic panel, HbA1c, lipid panel — to identify contraindications and establish a clinical baseline before medication starts
  • Structured dose titration: Semaglutide is initiated at 0.25mg weekly and increased on a monthly schedule to minimize nausea, vomiting, and GI side effects — the primary reason patients discontinue
  • Ongoing monitoring: Follow-up visits to assess response, manage side effects, and adjust the plan if weight loss plateaus
  • Clinical judgment on medication selection: Some patients may be better candidates for tirzepatide (Zepbound) than semaglutide. SURMOUNT 1 showed average 22.5% body weight loss with tirzepatide 15mg over 72 weeks. A physician evaluation is needed to determine which medication fits your clinical profile.

Telehealth-only platforms can provide legitimate access for patients unable to travel. However, the 25-mile distance from Oak Ridge to the Knoxville service area is manageable for most patients, and in-person evaluation adds clinical value beyond a video intake call.
