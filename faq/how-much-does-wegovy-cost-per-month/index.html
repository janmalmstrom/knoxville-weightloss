Cost & Insurance

How much does Wegovy cost per month?

Quick Answer

Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) has a list price of approximately $1,349 per month without insurance coverage. With commercial insurance that covers obesity medications, out-of-pocket costs can drop to $0–$200 per month depending on your plan; the Novo Nordisk savings card can reduce costs to as low as $0 per month for eligible commercially insured patients. Medicare and most Medicaid plans do not currently cover Wegovy for weight loss alone, though the landscape is changing.

Key Takeaways

  • Wegovy's list price is approximately $1,349/month — but most patients with commercial insurance who have obesity-related comorbidities pay significantly less after manufacturer savings programs.
  • The Novo Nordisk NovoCare savings card can reduce Wegovy costs to $0/month for eligible commercially insured patients; uninsured patients may pay $500–$650/month through the program.
  • Medicare Part D currently does not cover Wegovy solely for weight loss under existing law, though pending federal legislation may change this.
  • Compounded semaglutide is cheaper upfront but is not FDA-approved and carries quality, dosing, and regulatory risks that physician-supervised brand-name therapy does not.
  • Physician-supervised GLP-1 therapy at a clinic includes monitoring, lab work, and dose titration — costs that factor into the true value comparison with direct-to-consumer options.

Wegovy's List Price: What the Sticker Says vs. What Patients Actually Pay

The manufacturer list price for Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg, manufactured by Novo Nordisk) is approximately $1,349 per month for a four-pen supply in the United States. This figure reflects the wholesale acquisition cost and is what you would pay if you purchased Wegovy without insurance and without any manufacturer assistance program.

That number can be alarming. It is also rarely what most commercially insured patients in Knoxville actually pay at the pharmacy counter. The real cost depends on three variables: whether your insurance plan covers obesity medications, whether you qualify for the Novo Nordisk NovoCare savings program, and whether you are on Medicare or Medicaid rather than commercial insurance. Each scenario produces a dramatically different monthly cost, which is why "how much does Wegovy cost" cannot be answered with a single number — it requires walking through each payer category.

What is not negotiable is the medication's clinical value. In the STEP 1 trial (Wilding et al., 2021, New England Journal of Medicine), semaglutide 2.4mg produced an average body weight reduction of 14.9% over 68 weeks. That level of efficacy — significantly greater than any previously available non-surgical obesity intervention — is the benchmark against which monthly cost should be evaluated.

Commercial Insurance Coverage: When Wegovy Is Affordable

Commercial insurance coverage for Wegovy varies considerably by employer plan and insurer. Wegovy is FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or a BMI of 27 or higher with at least one weight-related comorbidity such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes, or high cholesterol. Plans that cover obesity medications — which increasingly includes large employer plans under pressure to address workforce health costs — typically require prior authorization confirming you meet these BMI criteria.

When commercial insurance covers Wegovy, typical patient out-of-pocket costs range from $0 to $200 per month depending on your plan's formulary tier and copay structure. Novo Nordisk's NovoCare savings card can further reduce costs: eligible commercially insured patients may pay as little as $0 per month (with a cap on total annual savings). Patients without insurance who meet certain income criteria can access Wegovy through the Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program at reduced or no cost. It is worth calling your insurer directly before your first prescription to confirm coverage and understand prior authorization requirements — your physician's office at Knoxville Weight Loss Clinic can assist with this documentation.

Some commercial plans in Tennessee cover Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg) for type 2 diabetes but do not cover Wegovy for obesity. It is important to understand that Ozempic and Wegovy are different FDA-approved products: Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes management; its use for weight loss is off-label. Wegovy is FDA-approved specifically for chronic weight management. They are not interchangeable from a coverage standpoint.

Medicare and Medicaid: The Coverage Gap — and What's Changing

Medicare Part D currently does not cover Wegovy or any GLP-1 medication prescribed solely for weight loss. This restriction traces to the Medicare Modernization Act of 2003, which explicitly excluded drugs used for "weight loss" from Part D coverage. This means that for the millions of Medicare beneficiaries who are overweight or obese, Wegovy's $1,349 list price is largely unsubsidized — a significant access barrier.

There is movement on this front. The Treat and Reduce Obesity Act, introduced in multiple Congressional sessions, would authorize Medicare to cover FDA-approved obesity medications including Wegovy. Additionally, the SELECT trial (2023) demonstrated that semaglutide 2.4mg reduced major cardiovascular events — heart attack, stroke, or cardiovascular death — by 20% in overweight and obese patients without diabetes over a mean follow-up of 34 months. This cardiovascular outcome data has strengthened the policy argument that Wegovy is not a cosmetic drug but a medically necessary intervention for high-risk patients.

As of this writing, Medicare Part D coverage rules remain unchanged. Tennessee Medicaid (TennCare) coverage for obesity medications is limited and plan-dependent. If you are a Medicare or TennCare beneficiary, discuss your specific situation with our clinical team — there are prior authorization pathways and appeal options worth exploring, particularly if you have documented cardiovascular risk or metabolic comorbidities. Information on ACA marketplace plan requirements is available at healthcare.gov, and Medicare Part D coverage rules are detailed at cms.gov.

Compounded Semaglutide: Lower Price, Different Risk Profile

Compounded semaglutide has been widely marketed as a lower-cost alternative to Wegovy, with prices typically ranging from $200 to $400 per month through telehealth prescribers. The appeal is obvious. The risks, however, are clinically significant and should be understood before making a cost-based decision.

Compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved. It was permitted under 503A and 503B pharmacy compounding provisions while Wegovy was listed on the FDA drug shortage list. As Novo Nordisk has addressed supply constraints, the FDA has taken enforcement action against compounders offering semaglutide outside the shortage framework. Compounded products are not subject to the same manufacturing quality standards as FDA-approved drugs — potency, sterility, and excipient composition may vary. There have been reports of dosing errors and adverse events associated with compounded GLP-1 formulations.

At Knoxville Weight Loss Clinic, our physician-supervised program uses FDA-approved Wegovy or Zepbound — not compounded alternatives. The reason is clinical, not commercial: the STEP and SURMOUNT trial safety and efficacy data that informs our dosing protocols was generated using FDA-approved formulations. When you choose physician-supervised therapy with brand-name medications, you are choosing the evidence base that produced those clinical outcomes.

The Real Cost Calculation: What Physician Supervision Adds to the Value Equation

Monthly medication cost is one component of the total cost of GLP-1 therapy. Physician-supervised programs also include services that direct-to-consumer telehealth prescribers may not provide: baseline laboratory work (thyroid function, metabolic panel, HbA1c), monthly dose titration visits, side effect management, and monitoring for contraindications including a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2.

These are not administrative add-ons — they are the clinical infrastructure that makes GLP-1 therapy safe and produces outcomes comparable to the STEP 1 trial results. Semaglutide requires a structured dose escalation schedule (starting at 0.25mg weekly and titrating to 2.4mg over approximately 16–20 weeks) to minimize gastrointestinal side effects. Without physician oversight, patients who discontinue early due to unmanaged nausea never reach the therapeutic dose that produces 15% body weight reduction.

The monthly cost of Wegovy with physician supervision at Knoxville Weight Loss Clinic is best understood as the cost of treating a chronic metabolic disease — one associated, when untreated, with type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, sleep apnea, and reduced life expectancy. Tennessee's obesity rate of approximately 36% is among the highest in the nation. For many East Tennessee patients, the question is not whether they can afford treatment — it is whether they can afford to continue without it. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the exact monthly list price for Wegovy in 2024?
Wegovy's current wholesale list price is approximately $1,349 per month for a four-pen supply (one injection per week). This is the price without insurance or manufacturer assistance. Most commercially insured patients pay significantly less — often $0–$200 per month — depending on their plan's coverage for obesity medications and eligibility for the Novo Nordisk NovoCare savings program.
Does insurance cover Wegovy for weight loss?
Coverage depends on your specific plan. Wegovy is FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults with BMI ≥30, or BMI ≥27 with a weight-related comorbidity, and an increasing number of commercial employer plans cover it with prior authorization. Medicare Part D does not currently cover Wegovy prescribed solely for weight loss under existing federal law, though pending legislation may change this. Confirming your coverage before starting therapy is an important first step our clinical team can help with.
Does Medicare cover Wegovy or semaglutide for weight loss?
No — Medicare Part D does not currently cover GLP-1 medications when prescribed solely for weight loss, due to restrictions in the Medicare Modernization Act of 2003. The SELECT trial's 2023 finding that semaglutide reduced major cardiovascular events by 20% in overweight and obese patients without diabetes has intensified policy debate about this restriction. Patients with type 2 diabetes who also need weight loss may have a different coverage pathway through Ozempic's diabetes indication — discuss this with your physician. Details on current Medicare Part D rules are available at cms.gov.
How much does Wegovy cost without insurance?
Without insurance, the list price is approximately $1,349 per month. Uninsured patients who do not qualify for the NovoCare savings card may pay this full amount at retail pharmacies. Novo Nordisk does offer a patient assistance program for qualifying uninsured patients based on income — contacting NovoCare directly or through our clinic team can help determine eligibility. Compounded semaglutide is available at lower cost but is not FDA-approved and carries quality and regulatory risks not present with the brand-name product.
Is Ozempic cheaper than Wegovy, and can I use it for weight loss instead?
Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg) and Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) have similar list prices — Ozempic is approximately $935–$1,000 per month. Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; its use for weight loss is an established off-label application, not an FDA-approved indication. Wegovy delivers a higher therapeutic dose (2.4mg versus a maximum 2mg for Ozempic) that was specifically studied for weight management in the STEP trials, producing average 14.9% body weight loss. Using Ozempic for weight loss is a clinical decision that requires physician oversight, and coverage rules differ from Wegovy.
How does compounded semaglutide compare in cost to Wegovy?
Compounded semaglutide is typically priced at $200–$400 per month — substantially below Wegovy's list price. However, compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved, was only permitted during the Wegovy drug shortage period, and is not manufactured to the same quality standards as the FDA-approved product. The FDA has taken enforcement action against compounders offering semaglutide as shortages have resolved. The clinical trial evidence supporting semaglutide's 14.9% average weight loss (STEP 1) was generated using the FDA-approved formulation, not compounded versions.
What does a Wegovy savings card or coupon actually cover?
Novo Nordisk's NovoCare savings program for Wegovy offers eligible commercially insured patients a copay card that can reduce monthly costs to as low as $0, subject to annual savings caps and eligibility requirements. Patients must have commercial insurance that covers Wegovy — the savings card cannot be used with Medicare, Medicaid, or other federal or state government insurance programs. Eligibility and program terms can change, so verifying current terms at the time of prescription is recommended; our clinical team can assist with this process.
Are there ACA marketplace plans that cover Wegovy?
ACA marketplace plans are not required by federal law to cover obesity medications as an essential health benefit, though some plans voluntarily include coverage. Coverage varies substantially by state, insurer, and plan tier. The ACA does require coverage of obesity counseling and screening as preventive services, but this is distinct from medication coverage. Patients selecting marketplace plans at healthcare.gov can review each plan's formulary before enrolling — searching for "semaglutide" or "Wegovy" in the drug lookup tool will show whether a given plan covers the medication and at what tier.
