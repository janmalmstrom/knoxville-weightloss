What happens at a GLP-1 weight loss consultation?

Quick Answer

A GLP-1 weight loss consultation is a structured medical evaluation — not a sales appointment — in which a physician reviews your health history, current medications, lab work, and BMI to determine whether semaglutide (Wegovy) or tirzepatide (Zepbound) is medically appropriate for you. If you qualify, the visit results in a personalized treatment plan including starting dose, titration schedule, and monitoring intervals. Most initial consultations run 45 to 60 minutes.

Key Takeaways

  • Eligibility is determined by BMI threshold (≥30, or ≥27 with a weight-related comorbidity such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes, or high cholesterol) — the same criteria used in the STEP and SURMOUNT clinical trials.
  • Baseline lab work — typically a metabolic panel, thyroid function, and HbA1c — is ordered at or before the first visit to establish safety parameters and rule out contraindications.
  • The physician will review your medication list carefully, since several drug classes interact with GLP-1 receptor agonists, particularly insulin and sulfonylureas in patients with type 2 diabetes.
  • You will leave with a documented dose titration plan — GLP-1 medications are started at a low dose and increased incrementally over weeks to months to minimize nausea and other gastrointestinal side effects.
  • Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) and Zepbound (tirzepatide) are the FDA-approved options for chronic weight management; if your physician discusses Ozempic or Mounjaro, those carry FDA approval for type 2 diabetes — weight loss is an established but off-label use of both.

Why a Formal Consultation Matters Before Starting GLP-1 Therapy

GLP-1 receptor agonists — semaglutide and tirzepatide — are among the most effective pharmacological interventions for obesity ever studied. The STEP 1 trial (Wilding et al., 2021, NEJM) showed an average 14.9% reduction in body weight with semaglutide 2.4mg over 68 weeks. The SURMOUNT 1 trial (Jastreboff et al., 2022, NEJM) showed an average 22.5% reduction with tirzepatide 15mg over 72 weeks. Those results, however, were produced under physician-supervised, protocol-driven conditions — not unsupervised prescription access.

A formal consultation exists to replicate that oversight. It gives your physician the information needed to confirm you are a candidate, select the right medication and starting dose, identify contraindications before they become problems, and establish a monitoring schedule that keeps the therapy working safely over time. Skipping this step — as some direct-to-consumer telehealth models effectively do — means managing a potent medication without the clinical baseline required to do it well.

The Obesity Medicine Association identifies comprehensive evaluation as a foundational standard of obesity care, not a formality. At Knoxville Weight Loss Clinic, the consultation is where your treatment actually begins.

What to Expect During the Medical History and Eligibility Assessment

The first portion of the consultation is a structured medical history review. Your physician is gathering information across several domains simultaneously: weight history, prior weight loss attempts, current comorbidities, medications, family history, and any conditions that would contraindicate GLP-1 therapy.

Eligibility follows FDA-established criteria, which mirror the enrollment criteria used in the landmark clinical trials:

  • BMI ≥30 (obesity), or
  • BMI ≥27 (overweight) plus at least one weight-related comorbidity — hypertension, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea, or cardiovascular disease

Your physician will also screen for contraindications. GLP-1 receptor agonists carry an FDA boxed warning regarding a potential risk of thyroid C-cell tumors observed in rodent studies. Patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2) are not candidates. A history of pancreatitis is also reviewed carefully. Patients who are pregnant or planning pregnancy are not candidates for GLP-1 weight loss therapy.

Your medication list matters significantly. Patients with type 2 diabetes who are taking insulin or sulfonylureas require dose adjustment before starting a GLP-1 agent to reduce hypoglycemia risk. The NIDDK emphasizes that coordinated medication management is essential when treating obesity alongside diabetes.

Lab Work: What Gets Ordered and Why

A baseline laboratory panel is a standard component of physician-supervised GLP-1 therapy. It serves two purposes: ruling out contraindications and establishing a baseline against which future results can be compared to track the metabolic benefits of treatment.

The standard baseline panel typically includes:

  • Comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP) — evaluates kidney function, liver enzymes, electrolytes, and blood glucose. Elevated liver enzymes may prompt additional evaluation before starting therapy.
  • Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) — screens for thyroid dysfunction and provides baseline data given the thyroid-related boxed warning associated with GLP-1 medications.
  • HbA1c — identifies undiagnosed type 2 diabetes or prediabetes, which affects both medication selection and the monitoring schedule going forward.
  • Fasting lipid panel — establishes cardiovascular risk profile. This is especially relevant given the SELECT trial data showing semaglutide reduced major cardiovascular events by 20% in overweight and obese patients without diabetes.

Labs ordered at the initial visit are typically repeated at 3- to 6-month intervals to monitor for treatment response and safety. Patients who start GLP-1 therapy and achieve meaningful weight loss often see measurable improvements in blood pressure, HbA1c, and lipid levels — findings consistent with published STEP trial secondary endpoints.

Medication Selection: Semaglutide vs. Tirzepatide — and What the FDA Status Means for You

One of the most clinically significant parts of the consultation is medication selection. Your physician will discuss the two FDA-approved options for chronic weight management — and clarify the regulatory status of related medications you may have heard about.

Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) received FDA approval for chronic weight management in June 2021. It is a GLP-1 receptor agonist that reduces appetite by acting on hypothalamic hunger centers and slows gastric emptying. Average weight loss in the STEP 1 trial was 14.9% over 68 weeks.

Zepbound (tirzepatide 2.5–15mg) received FDA approval for chronic weight management in November 2023. It acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors — a dual mechanism associated with stronger efficacy in trial data. Average weight loss in SURMOUNT 1 was 22.5% at the highest dose over 72 weeks.

Your physician may also discuss Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg) or Mounjaro (tirzepatide) — both FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, with weight loss representing an established but off-label use. If you have type 2 diabetes, one of these may be the medically appropriate choice, with the weight loss benefit an expected secondary outcome. Your physician will document the clinical rationale either way.

Compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved and should be discussed transparently with your physician if you have encountered it. Its regulatory status has been subject to ongoing FDA action as branded drug shortages have resolved.

Leaving the Consultation: Your Dose Titration Plan and Follow-Up Schedule

A physician-supervised consultation does not end with a prescription — it ends with a documented plan. You should leave the visit with clear information on each of the following:

  • Starting dose — both Wegovy and Zepbound are initiated at the lowest available dose to allow your body to adapt and minimize gastrointestinal side effects. Semaglutide begins at 0.25mg weekly; tirzepatide begins at 2.5mg weekly.
  • Titration schedule — doses are increased incrementally, typically every four weeks, toward the target therapeutic dose. The titration schedule used in the STEP trials was gradual by design — rapid dose escalation increases nausea and discontinuation rates.
  • Side effect management — nausea, constipation, and mild gastrointestinal discomfort are common, especially in the first weeks of therapy. Your physician will explain what is expected versus what warrants a call to the clinic.
  • Follow-up interval — monthly contact is standard during active dose titration. Once you reach a stable maintenance dose, intervals may extend to quarterly visits with intervening lab work.
  • Behavioral support — the clinical trial protocols that produced the STEP and SURMOUNT efficacy data included structured behavioral counseling alongside medication. Your physician will discuss dietary approaches and activity guidance as part of a comprehensive plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the initial GLP-1 consultation take?
Most initial consultations run 45 to 60 minutes. The physician needs adequate time to review your medical history, current medications, and weight history, and to explain the treatment options and what to expect during titration. Rushed evaluations — common in some direct-to-consumer telehealth models — compromise the quality of clinical decision-making.
Do I need lab work before my first appointment, or can I get it ordered at the visit?
This varies by clinic. At a physician-supervised clinic, lab work may be ordered at the initial visit or in advance, depending on whether your prior labs are recent and available. Either way, baseline labs — including a metabolic panel, TSH, HbA1c, and lipid panel — are a standard component of GLP-1 therapy initiation, not optional.
What BMI do I need to qualify for semaglutide or tirzepatide?
The FDA-approved indications for Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) and Zepbound (tirzepatide) require a BMI of 30 or higher, or a BMI of 27 or higher with at least one weight-related comorbidity such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes, or high cholesterol. These thresholds mirror the enrollment criteria used in the STEP and SURMOUNT clinical trials.
Will the physician ask about my past diet attempts?
Yes, and the framing matters. A physician practicing obesity medicine treats obesity as a chronic metabolic disease — not a result of insufficient effort. Your history of prior diet and exercise attempts provides clinical context, and the physician's role is not to re-litigate those efforts but to explain why GLP-1 therapy addresses the biological drivers of hunger and weight regulation that behavioral approaches alone cannot fully overcome.
Is Ozempic the same as Wegovy? Will the consultation clarify which one I should be on?
Ozempic and Wegovy both contain semaglutide, but at different doses and with different FDA approvals. Wegovy (2.4mg weekly) is FDA-approved for chronic weight management. Ozempic (0.5–2mg) is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes — weight loss is an established but off-label use. Your physician will determine which is medically appropriate based on whether you have a diabetes diagnosis and your overall clinical picture.
What happens if I have type 2 diabetes and I'm also hoping to lose weight?
Patients with type 2 diabetes are strong candidates for GLP-1 therapy and often have the most to gain from it. Your physician will review your current diabetes medications carefully — particularly insulin and sulfonylureas, which may require dose reduction to prevent hypoglycemia when starting a GLP-1 agent. The NIDDK and Obesity Medicine Association both identify GLP-1 receptor agonists as first-line pharmacotherapy for patients with obesity and type 2 diabetes.
What are the main contraindications that would disqualify me at the consultation?
The primary contraindications reviewed at the consultation include a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2), both of which appear in the FDA boxed warning for GLP-1 receptor agonists. Current pregnancy or planned pregnancy in the near term is also a contraindication. A history of pancreatitis warrants careful clinical judgment. These are not bureaucratic checkboxes — they are clinically meaningful safety screens.
How soon after the consultation can I start the medication?
If lab work has already been completed and reviewed, some patients can begin the same week as their consultation. If labs are ordered at the first visit, there may be a brief window while results are returned and reviewed by the physician. Once cleared, the prescription is initiated at the lowest starting dose — 0.25mg weekly for semaglutide or 2.5mg weekly for tirzepatide — with the first dose titration occurring at approximately four weeks.
