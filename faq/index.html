How GLP-1 Works

What is a GLP-1 receptor agonist and how does it cause weight loss?

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a hormone naturally produced in the gut after eating. It signals the brain to reduce appetite, triggers the pancreas to release insulin, and slows gastric emptying so food moves through the stomach more slowly. GLP-1 receptor agonists are synthetic molecules that mimic this hormone — but at higher, sustained concentrations than the body produces naturally.

The result: reduced hunger signals in the hypothalamus, earlier and prolonged feelings of fullness after eating, and improved blood sugar regulation. Weight loss occurs because patients eat significantly less without the hunger, cravings, or sense of deprivation that drives most diet failures.

Why does GLP-1 therapy work when diet and exercise didn't?

Diet and exercise address behavior. GLP-1 therapy addresses the biology that drives the behavior. When you lose weight through calorie restriction alone, the body compensates: hunger hormones increase, metabolism slows, and cravings intensify. This is why most people who lose significant weight through diet regain it — the biology actively works against the behavioral change.

GLP-1 medications work at the level of the hypothalamus — the brain's appetite regulation center — resetting the hunger set point rather than fighting it. Patients consistently report that food simply becomes less important. Not willpower over cravings; the cravings diminish.

How is tirzepatide different from semaglutide mechanistically?

Semaglutide activates only the GLP-1 receptor. Tirzepatide is a dual agonist — it activates both the GLP-1 receptor and the GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) receptor. GIP is another incretin hormone involved in fat metabolism and insulin secretion.

The addition of GIP agonism appears to amplify the appetite-suppressing and metabolic effects beyond what GLP-1 alone achieves. This likely explains why tirzepatide produces greater average weight loss (20–22%) than semaglutide (15%) in clinical trials. The dual mechanism is a genuine pharmacological difference, not marketing.

Semaglutide / Wegovy

What is the difference between Ozempic, Wegovy, and generic semaglutide?

Semaglutide is the active drug. The brand name depends on the dose and indication:

  • Ozempic (0.5mg, 1mg, 2mg): FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes (December 2017). Weight loss is an established off-label effect — not the approved indication.
  • Wegovy (2.4mg): FDA-approved for chronic weight management (June 2021). This is the weight loss indication.
  • Compounded semaglutide: Not FDA-approved. Made by compounding pharmacies under shortage provisions. Varies in purity and potency.
  • Generic semaglutide: Does not exist as an FDA-approved product. Any product marketed as generic semaglutide is a compounded product.

The 2.4mg dose used in Wegovy is higher than Ozempic's maximum dose — this higher dose is part of why weight loss outcomes are greater than what is typically seen with Ozempic off-label.

What results should I expect from semaglutide?

The STEP 1 clinical trial (2021, NEJM) showed semaglutide 2.4mg produced an average 14.9% body weight reduction over 68 weeks. For a patient starting at 250 lbs, that is approximately 37 lbs. About one-third of participants lost more than 20% of body weight.

Individual results vary significantly. Most patients begin noticing reduced appetite within the first 2–4 weeks at the starting dose. Measurable weight loss typically becomes evident by week 8–12. Maximum results are usually achieved around week 52–68.

Semaglutide is not a cure for obesity. The STEP 4 trial showed that participants who stopped semaglutide regained approximately two-thirds of their lost weight within a year. Long-term treatment is the standard approach for most patients.

How long does it take for semaglutide to start working?

Appetite reduction is often noticeable within the first week or two at the starting dose (0.25mg). However, weight loss at lower doses is minimal — the therapeutic weight loss doses are 1.7mg and 2.4mg, which most patients reach by weeks 13–17 following the standard titration schedule.

The titration schedule is not optional — it exists to allow the body to adapt and reduce nausea. Jumping to higher doses faster does not accelerate long-term weight loss and substantially increases side effect burden and medication abandonment.

Tirzepatide / Zepbound

Is Zepbound better than Wegovy for weight loss?

Clinical trial data shows tirzepatide (Zepbound) produces greater average weight loss than semaglutide (Wegovy): 20–22% in the SURMOUNT trials versus 15% in the STEP trials. In a 2023 head-to-head comparison study (SURMOUNT-5), tirzepatide produced approximately 47% more weight loss than semaglutide at comparable doses.

That said, clinical trials report averages. Individual response varies. Some patients respond very well to semaglutide and don't need the additional efficacy of tirzepatide. Factors including your medical history, other medications, insurance coverage, and cost all influence which medication is the right choice. A physician consultation is the correct way to make this decision.

What is the difference between Mounjaro and Zepbound?

Both Mounjaro and Zepbound contain tirzepatide as the active drug. The brand name reflects the FDA-approved indication:

  • Mounjaro: FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes (May 2022). Weight loss is off-label.
  • Zepbound: FDA-approved for chronic weight management (November 2023).

The medications are pharmacologically identical. The distinction matters primarily for insurance purposes: if weight loss is the goal, Zepbound is the correct prescription. Insurance coverage for Mounjaro for weight loss may differ from coverage for Zepbound.

GLP-1 for Patients with Type 2 Diabetes

Can I use Ozempic for weight loss if I have type 2 diabetes?

Yes — off-label prescribing is legal and common in standard medical practice. Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, and weight loss is a well-documented effect. Physicians routinely prescribe medications for purposes outside their FDA indication when evidence supports it.

For patients with type 2 diabetes seeking weight loss, the physician may also consider whether Wegovy (higher-dose semaglutide approved for weight management) or Zepbound (tirzepatide) is more appropriate, depending on current glycemic control, other medications, and insurance situation.

Will GLP-1 medications improve my blood sugar as well as help me lose weight?

For patients with type 2 diabetes, GLP-1 medications typically improve both glycemic control and body weight. The two effects reinforce each other — weight loss improves insulin sensitivity, which further improves blood sugar. Many patients see HbA1c reductions of 1–2% or more alongside significant weight loss.

This does mean patients on diabetes medications need careful monitoring when adding a GLP-1 — insulin doses may need to be reduced as blood sugar improves to avoid hypoglycemia. We coordinate with your endocrinologist or primary care physician when managing this.

The Treatment Experience

How are semaglutide and tirzepatide administered?

Both medications are weekly subcutaneous injections — injected under the skin, typically in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. The injection devices are pre-filled auto-injectors (similar to an EpiPen) that are designed for self-administration. Injection training is included in your initial appointment.

The injections involve a very fine needle and are generally well-tolerated. Most patients report minimal discomfort. The injection itself takes about 10 seconds.

Do I need to follow a specific diet while on semaglutide or tirzepatide?

There is no required diet protocol, but the medications work best when combined with reduced-calorie eating and increased physical activity. The GLP-1 mechanism reduces appetite substantially — most patients find it significantly easier to eat less without experiencing the hunger and cravings that usually accompany caloric restriction.

Common practical adjustments: eating slower, stopping when full (the satiety signal is stronger), and avoiding high-fat meals that can worsen nausea. Alcohol tolerance often decreases. Protein intake is important to preserve muscle mass during rapid weight loss.

Safety & Side Effects

What are the most common side effects of GLP-1 medications?

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal:

  • Nausea: Most common, especially in the first 4–8 weeks and after dose increases. Usually improves with adaptation.
  • Vomiting: Less common than nausea; often dose-related.
  • Constipation: Common; increased fluid intake and fiber help.
  • Diarrhea: Less common than constipation but occurs.
  • Fatigue: Particularly early in treatment.

The standard titration schedule — starting at low doses and increasing slowly — is specifically designed to minimize these effects. Most patients find side effects manageable and diminishing after 4–12 weeks.

Is there a risk of thyroid cancer with semaglutide or tirzepatide?

GLP-1 receptor agonists carry a black box warning for thyroid C-cell tumors based on animal studies (rodents) that showed dose-dependent tumor development. This has not been demonstrated in humans in clinical trials or post-marketing surveillance to date.

However, the medications are contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2 (MEN2) — this is a firm exclusion criterion. Baseline thyroid screening is included in our initial lab work for all patients. Any new neck mass, difficulty swallowing, or persistent hoarseness while on treatment warrants immediate evaluation.

Does insurance cover weight loss medications in Tennessee?

Coverage varies significantly by plan and employer. General landscape as of 2026:

  • Commercial insurance: Some plans cover with prior authorization. Typically requires documented BMI criteria and prior behavioral intervention attempts.
  • Medicare Part D: Expanded coverage in 2026 for patients with established cardiovascular disease (SELECT trial data drove this change).
  • TennCare (Tennessee Medicaid): Coverage varies. Verify with your plan.
  • Self-insured employers: Increasingly covering due to productivity and downstream healthcare cost data. Some major Tennessee employers have added coverage.

We assist with prior authorization paperwork and can provide documentation of medical necessity. Even when coverage exists, formulary restrictions and step therapy requirements vary — we help navigate this.

Choosing a GLP-1 Clinic

What is the difference between a telehealth GLP-1 service and a physician-supervised clinic?

Telehealth GLP-1 services primarily provide a prescription — often with a brief questionnaire and a quick async physician review. Prescriptions are delivered, and follow-up is minimal unless initiated by the patient.

A physician-supervised clinic provides:

  • Full medical history evaluation by a physician who reviews your case
  • Baseline lab work (thyroid, metabolic, HbA1c) before starting
  • Structured titration monitoring — monthly check-ins to review weight and side effects
  • Active management of side effects (dose adjustment, not just dismissal)
  • Coordination with your other providers (endocrinologist, cardiologist, etc.)
  • Prior authorization support for insurance coverage

The medication works. The physician oversight determines how safely and effectively it works for you specifically.

Should I use compounded semaglutide to save money?

Compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved. It was permitted under drug shortage provisions while branded semaglutide was unavailable. As the FDA has resolved shortage designations, it has moved to restrict compounding — meaning access will continue to narrow.

The practical risks: compounding pharmacies are not subject to the same manufacturing standards as FDA-approved pharmaceutical manufacturers. Potency, purity, and sterility vary. There have been documented cases of dosing errors and contamination with compounded injectables generally.

We prescribe FDA-approved Wegovy and Zepbound only. If cost is the primary barrier, we help patients navigate manufacturer savings programs and insurance prior authorization to reduce out-of-pocket cost on FDA-approved medications.
