Choosing a GLP-1 Clinic

Should I use a telehealth GLP-1 service or a physician supervised clinic?

By Ashley Morgan

Quick Answer

Physician-supervised clinics provide lab monitoring, dose titration, and side-effect management that telehealth-only GLP-1 services typically omit — a meaningful clinical difference for long-term safety and outcomes.

Key Takeaways

  • Telehealth GLP-1 services often issue prescriptions after a brief intake form; they rarely include baseline lab work, thyroid screening, or in-person clinical assessment.
  • STEP 1 trial participants receiving semaglutide 2.4mg achieved an average 14.9% body weight loss over 68 weeks — but that result came with structured monthly monitoring, not a one-time prescription.
  • Tennessee's obesity rate of approximately 36% is among the highest in the United States, yet access to board-certified obesity medicine physicians in East Tennessee remains limited — making it critical to distinguish between true medical supervision and prescription-only services.
  • Physician-supervised clinics should include baseline labs (metabolic panel, HbA1c, thyroid function), a documented titration schedule, and a protocol for managing common GLP-1 side effects such as nausea, vomiting, and gastroparesis risk.

What Does "Physician-Supervised" Actually Mean — and Why Does It Matter?

The phrase "physician-supervised" gets used loosely in GLP-1 weight loss marketing. In a clinically meaningful program, physician supervision includes four specific components: a baseline clinical assessment (including BMI, blood pressure, and relevant history), laboratory work before prescribing (at minimum a metabolic panel, HbA1c, and thyroid function), a structured dose titration schedule, and active management if side effects occur or the medication needs to be changed. A telehealth service that sends a questionnaire and issues a Wegovy or tirzepatide prescription within 24 hours is providing prescribing access — not supervision.

That distinction matters because GLP-1 receptor agonists are not risk-free. Contraindications include a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 2 (MEN2). Semaglutide and tirzepatide both carry an FDA boxed warning for this risk. A provider who relies solely on a self-reported intake form may miss contraindications that a thorough evaluation would catch.

💡 Clinical note: The Obesity Medicine Association (obesitymedicine.org) maintains a searchable directory of board-certified obesity medicine physicians. When evaluating any GLP-1 provider in Knoxville or East Tennessee, confirming ABOM (American Board of Obesity Medicine) certification is a reasonable starting point for vetting clinical credentials.

What Telehealth GLP-1 Services Do Well — and Where They Fall Short

Telehealth-only GLP-1 services have meaningfully expanded access to semaglutide and tirzepatide, particularly for patients in rural East Tennessee communities — from Oak Ridge to Morristown to Sevierville — where in-person obesity medicine specialists may not be geographically accessible. For a patient who is otherwise healthy, has no significant comorbidities, and is seeking Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg, FDA-approved for chronic weight management since June 2021) or Zepbound (tirzepatide, FDA-approved for chronic weight management since November 2023), a well-structured telehealth program can be a legitimate entry point.

Where telehealth services commonly fall short:

  • No baseline laboratory work. Many direct-to-consumer GLP-1 platforms do not require or coordinate lab testing before prescribing. A physician-supervised clinic should obtain at minimum a metabolic panel, HbA1c (to assess for undiagnosed type 2 diabetes), and thyroid function tests at baseline.
  • Compounded semaglutide without adequate disclosure. Several telehealth platforms have prescribed compounded semaglutide — which is not FDA-approved and was only permissible under drug shortage provisions that the FDA has been progressively restricting. Patients deserve clear disclosure that they are receiving a compounded product, not Wegovy or Ozempic.
  • No structured titration protocol. The STEP 1 trial titration schedule for semaglutide 2.4mg — escalating from 0.25mg over 16–20 weeks — was designed specifically to reduce gastrointestinal side effects. Platforms that accelerate dose escalation to improve perceived efficacy are deviating from studied protocols.
  • Limited side-effect management. Nausea, vomiting, constipation, and — in some cases — more serious concerns such as pancreatitis or gallbladder disease require clinical judgment to manage. Asynchronous messaging through a prescription platform is not equivalent to a provider relationship with real-time dose adjustment capability.
  • No cardiovascular risk stratification. The SELECT trial demonstrated that semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% in patients with overweight or obesity without diabetes — a landmark finding. It also underscores that GLP-1 candidates often carry cardiovascular risk that warrants clinical assessment, not just a BMI calculation.
⚠️ Important: If a telehealth platform is offering compounded semaglutide, ask specifically whether it is FDA-approved Wegovy or a compounded product. Compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved. The FDA has issued guidance restricting compounding as brand-name semaglutide shortages resolve. Patients should understand what they are receiving before starting treatment.

How a Physician-Supervised GLP-1 Clinic in Knoxville Approaches Treatment Differently

Knox County and the surrounding East Tennessee region reflect Tennessee's statewide obesity prevalence of approximately 36% — among the highest in the country according to CDC data. The patient population served by clinics near the University of Tennessee Medical Center and Covenant Health includes a high proportion of adults with obesity-related comorbidities: type 2 diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and obstructive sleep apnea. These patients require a clinical program, not simply a prescription.

A physician-supervised clinic offering semaglutide or tirzepatide should structure the program as follows:

What a Physician-Supervised GLP-1 Program Should Include

  • Initial clinical assessment: Full medical history, BMI, blood pressure, documentation of comorbidities, contraindication screening (MTC/MEN2 history, pancreatitis history, pregnancy status)
  • Baseline laboratory work: Comprehensive metabolic panel, HbA1c, lipid panel, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), and complete blood count at minimum
  • Medication selection: Clinical rationale for Wegovy vs. Zepbound — or, in patients with type 2 diabetes, Ozempic or Mounjaro for glycemic management with weight benefit — based on patient profile, insurance, and treatment goals. Ozempic and Mounjaro are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; prescribing them primarily for weight loss is off-label use.
  • Structured titration schedule: Monthly dose escalation with monitoring, consistent with STEP and SURMOUNT trial protocols
  • Side-effect management protocol: Documented approach for nausea, GI symptoms, and patient access to the provider when issues arise
  • Follow-up labs: Repeat metabolic panel and HbA1c at 3–6 months to monitor response and safety
  • Long-term plan: Discussion of medication duration, weight maintenance strategy, and management if the medication is discontinued

The licensed providers connected through Knoxville Weight Loss Clinic operate within this framework. The goal is not to issue a prescription — it is to manage a chronic metabolic disease, which is how obesity is classified by the American Medical Association and the Obesity Medicine Association.

Wegovy vs. Zepbound: Which Medication Does a Knoxville Provider Prescribe?

Both Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) and Zepbound (tirzepatide 2.5–15mg) are FDA-approved for chronic weight management. The clinical evidence for both should inform the prescribing decision:

  • Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg): In the STEP 1 trial, participants with obesity or overweight with at least one comorbidity achieved an average 14.9% reduction in body weight over 68 weeks (Wilding et al., 2021, NEJM). The SELECT trial demonstrated a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with overweight or obesity without diabetes — currently the only GLP-1 weight management agent with this cardiovascular outcomes indication.
  • Zepbound (tirzepatide 2.5–15mg): In the SURMOUNT-1 trial, participants receiving the 15mg dose achieved an average 22.5% reduction in body weight over 72 weeks (Jastreboff et al., 2022, NEJM). As a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, tirzepatide acts on two incretin pathways simultaneously — a mechanism thought to explain its numerically superior weight loss outcomes compared to semaglutide monotherapy, though head-to-head trial data remain limited.

The choice between the two depends on patient history (particularly cardiovascular risk, where semaglutide's SELECT data are currently unique), insurance coverage, cost, and prior response to GLP-1 therapy. This is a clinical decision a physician should make with the patient — not one that defaults to whichever product a platform has available.

💡 Clinical note: Ozempic and Mounjaro — the type 2 diabetes formulations of semaglutide and tirzepatide — are sometimes prescribed off-label for weight management, particularly in patients with type 2 diabetes who benefit from both glycemic control and weight reduction. This is an established and clinically appropriate off-label use. Patients should understand the distinction: Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, not for chronic weight management. Wegovy is the weight-management-approved semaglutide formulation.

Cost and Insurance: Does the Supervision Model Affect What You Pay?

One reason patients gravitate toward telehealth GLP-1 platforms is cost transparency. Many platforms advertise compounded semaglutide for $200–$400 per month with no insurance required. That price point is real — but it reflects a compounded product, not FDA-approved Wegovy or Zepbound, and it does not include lab work, follow-up visits, or clinical oversight.

FDA-approved Wegovy carries a list price of approximately $1,350 per month without insurance; Zepbound's list price is comparable. Manufacturer savings programs — Novo Nordisk's Wegovy savings card and Eli Lilly's Zepbound savings card — can reduce out-of-pocket costs substantially for commercially insured patients. Tennessee Medicaid (TennCare) coverage for GLP-1 weight management medications remains limited and variable. A physician-supervised clinic can assist with prior authorization documentation, including letters of medical necessity tied to documented comorbidities — support that prescription-only platforms typically do not provide.

Employer-sponsored insurance plans across East Tennessee vary considerably in GLP-1 coverage, including among employees of Oak Ridge National Laboratory, TVA, and the University of Tennessee system. A clinical provider familiar with the local insurance landscape can meaningfully affect what a patient ultimately pays out of pocket.

How to Evaluate Any GLP-1 Provider in Knoxville Before You Start

Whether considering a telehealth service or an in-person clinic, patients in Knoxville and across East Tennessee should ask the following questions before starting GLP-1 therapy:

  • Is the prescribing physician board-certified in obesity medicine, internal medicine, or endocrinology? (Search the Obesity Medicine Association directory at obesitymedicine.org.)
  • Will baseline lab work be completed before the first prescription?
  • Is the medication being prescribed FDA-approved Wegovy or Zepbound, or a compounded product? If compounded — why, and what are the risks?
  • What is the dose titration schedule, and how does it compare to the STEP or SURMOUNT trial protocols?
  • What happens if significant nausea, vomiting, or other side effects occur — who responds, and how quickly?
  • Will the provider assist with insurance prior authorization if needed?
  • Is there a long-term management plan, including guidance if the patient reaches their weight loss goal or needs to discontinue the medication?

A provider who cannot answer these questions clearly is not offering physician-supervised care in any meaningful clinical sense. Obesity is a chronic metabolic disease — it requires chronic disease management, not a one-time prescription.

Bottom Line

Telehealth GLP-1 services have improved access to semaglutide and tirzepatide, but access to a prescription is not the same as access to care. In East Tennessee — where obesity prevalence approaches 36% and many patients carry cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities — physician-supervised GLP-1 therapy that includes baseline labs, structured titration, and ongoing monitoring represents the appropriate standard of care. The STEP 1 trial demonstrated 14.9% average body weight loss with semaglutide 2.4mg over 68 weeks; SURMOUNT-1 demonstrated 22.5% with tirzepatide 15mg over 72 weeks — outcomes achieved in monitored clinical settings with structured protocols. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a telehealth GLP-1 service and a physician-supervised clinic?

A telehealth service primarily provides a prescription after an intake questionnaire; a physician-supervised clinic adds baseline lab work, structured dose titration, contraindication screening, and active side-effect management. The STEP 1 trial — which produced an average 14.9% body weight loss with semaglutide 2.4mg — was conducted with monthly monitoring, not one-time prescribing. The clinical standard reflects that supervised model.

Is compounded semaglutide the same as Wegovy?

No. Wegovy is an FDA-approved semaglutide product (2.4mg, approved June 2021 for chronic weight management). Compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved and was only permissible under drug shortage provisions — the FDA has been restricting compounding as brand-name shortages resolve. Patients receiving compounded semaglutide should be clearly informed they are not receiving an FDA-approved product.

Can I get semaglutide or tirzepatide without lab work?

Some telehealth platforms do not require baseline labs before prescribing. However, physician-supervised programs should include at minimum a metabolic panel, HbA1c, and TSH before initiating GLP-1 therapy, both to rule out contraindications and to document baseline metabolic status. Patients with undiagnosed type 2 diabetes or thyroid disease need this information before starting treatment.

Is Ozempic FDA-approved for weight loss?

No. Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg) is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes management (approved December 2017). Weight loss is an established off-label use for Ozempic. Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) is the FDA-approved formulation for chronic weight management. A physician who prescribes Ozempic for weight loss is using it off-label — which is clinically common and appropriate in many cases, but patients should understand the distinction.

Which produces more weight loss — semaglutide or tirzepatide?

In head-to-head context using trial data, tirzepatide shows numerically superior results. SURMOUNT-1 demonstrated 22.5% average body weight loss at tirzepatide 15mg over 72 weeks; STEP 1 showed 14.9% with semaglutide 2.4mg over 68 weeks. However, semaglutide currently holds the SELECT trial cardiovascular outcomes data — a 20% reduction in major cardiovascular events — which may be clinically decisive for patients with elevated CV risk.

Does Tennessee Medicaid (TennCare) cover Wegovy or Zepbound?

Coverage under TennCare for GLP-1 weight management medications is limited and variable. Commercially insured patients in East Tennessee — including those covered through UT system, ORNL, or TVA employer plans — should check their specific formulary. A physician-supervised clinic can prepare medical necessity documentation and prior authorization letters tied to documented comorbidities, which a prescription-only telehealth platform typically cannot provide.

How do I find a board-certified obesity medicine physician in Knoxville?

The Obesity Medicine Association maintains a searchable provider directory at obesitymedicine.org. Board certification through ABOM (American Board of Obesity Medicine) indicates a physician has completed focused training in obesity as a metabolic disease — beyond a general prescribing license. What should I expect from a first appointment at a physician-supervised GLP-1 clinic?

A thorough first visit includes a full medical history, BMI and blood pressure assessment, contraindication screening (particularly for medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2 history), baseline laboratory work, and a discussion of medication options — including the clinical differences between Wegovy and Zepbound. The provider should also review the dose titration schedule, set realistic expectations based on STEP and SURMOUNT trial data, and establish a plan for managing potential side effects before the first injection is administered.
