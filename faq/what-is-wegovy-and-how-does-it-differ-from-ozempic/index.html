Semaglutide & Wegovy

What is Wegovy and how does it differ from Ozempic?

By Ashley Morgan

Quick Answer

Wegovy and Ozempic both contain semaglutide, but Wegovy is FDA-approved for chronic weight management while Ozempic is FDA-approved only for type 2 diabetes — they differ in dose, approval, and clinical intent.

Key Takeaways

  • Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) received FDA approval for chronic weight management in June 2021; Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg) is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes only — weight loss use of Ozempic is off-label.
  • The STEP 1 trial (Wilding et al., NEJM 2021) showed semaglutide 2.4mg produced an average 14.9% reduction in body weight over 68 weeks in adults without diabetes.
  • Wegovy's maximum dose of 2.4mg is meaningfully higher than Ozempic's ceiling of 2mg, and that dose difference is central to its greater weight loss effect in the clinical trial data.
  • With Tennessee carrying one of the nation's highest obesity rates — approximately 36% of adults — physician-supervised semaglutide therapy represents a clinically significant intervention for a large portion of the Knoxville-area population.

What Is Wegovy, and What Was It Approved to Do?

Wegovy is the brand name for semaglutide 2.4mg, a once-weekly injectable medication manufactured by Novo Nordisk. The FDA approved it for chronic weight management in June 2021 — the first GLP-1 receptor agonist specifically approved for obesity treatment in adults. The indicated population: adults with a BMI of 30 or greater, or adults with a BMI of 27 or greater who have at least one weight-related comorbidity such as hypertension, dyslipidemia, or type 2 diabetes.

That approval reflects FDA review of weight-specific efficacy and safety data at the 2.4mg dose — a clinical threshold that matters. Wegovy is not a repurposed diabetes drug used off-label. Physicians in Knoxville prescribing it for obesity are working within a defined, on-label indication.

💡 Clinical note: Wegovy follows a titration schedule — patients begin at 0.25mg weekly and increase in 4-week intervals over 16–20 weeks to the 2.4mg maintenance dose. This gradual escalation is designed to reduce gastrointestinal side effects, not to delay therapeutic effect.

What Is Ozempic, and Why Do People Associate It With Weight Loss?

Ozempic contains semaglutide at doses of 0.5mg, 1mg, or 2mg. The FDA approved it in December 2017 for glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes, with an additional indication to reduce cardiovascular risk in that population. Prescribing Ozempic for weight loss in patients without type 2 diabetes is off-label use. That is legal and can be evidence-based — but patients deserve to know the distinction.

Ozempic does produce weight reduction; semaglutide's appetite-suppressing mechanism operates regardless of which branded formulation delivers it. The weight loss literature that drew widespread public attention, however — including the STEP trial program — was conducted using the 2.4mg dose now sold as Wegovy, not at Ozempic's lower ceiling dose.

⚠️ Important: Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes. Describing it as "FDA-approved for weight loss" is factually incorrect. Patients who encounter advertising framing it that way should ask their provider to clarify the approval status and the basis for the prescription.

How Do the Clinical Results Compare?

The STEP 1 trial (Wilding et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2021) remains the primary efficacy reference for semaglutide 2.4mg as a weight loss intervention. The trial enrolled 1,961 adults with obesity or overweight plus a comorbidity — none with type 2 diabetes — randomized to semaglutide 2.4mg weekly or placebo, both combined with lifestyle intervention. Over 68 weeks, participants on semaglutide achieved a mean body weight reduction of 14.9%, versus 2.4% with placebo. Approximately 86% of the semaglutide group lost at least 5% of body weight; roughly one-third lost 20% or more.

No comparable large-scale randomized trial has evaluated semaglutide at Ozempic's 2mg maximum dose specifically in a non-diabetic weight loss population. Dose-response data from the STEP program indicate that higher doses produce greater weight reduction — the clinical rationale behind the 2.4mg Wegovy target. Patients and providers weighing these two products on efficacy grounds should understand that the robust weight loss data was generated at the Wegovy dose.

Wegovy vs. Ozempic — Key Clinical Differences at a Glance

  • Active ingredient: Both contain semaglutide — the molecule is identical.
  • Wegovy dose: Titrates to 2.4mg weekly — the dose used in the STEP trials.
  • Ozempic dose: Maximum 2mg weekly — approved for type 2 diabetes management.
  • FDA approval — Wegovy: Chronic weight management (June 2021). BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with comorbidity.
  • FDA approval — Ozempic: Type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular risk reduction (December 2017). Weight loss = off-label.
  • Key efficacy data: STEP 1 trial — 14.9% mean body weight loss at 68 weeks with semaglutide 2.4mg (Wilding et al., NEJM 2021).
  • Cardiovascular data: The SELECT trial demonstrated a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events with semaglutide 2.4mg in overweight and obese adults without diabetes — a landmark finding for obesity medicine.
  • Insurance coverage: Wegovy coverage depends on whether a patient's plan includes weight management benefits. Ozempic coverage for off-label weight loss is frequently denied.

How Physician Supervision Changes the Equation

Semaglutide — as Wegovy or Ozempic — requires medical monitoring, not just a prescription. Titration decisions require clinical judgment: providers must assess whether gastrointestinal side effects warrant slowing dose escalation, identify contraindications such as personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, and evaluate interactions with concurrent medications. Baseline lab work — typically including a metabolic panel, HbA1c, and thyroid panel — is standard practice before initiating therapy at a physician-supervised clinic.

Bottom Line

Wegovy and Ozempic contain the same molecule — semaglutide — but are not interchangeable. Wegovy is FDA-approved for chronic weight management at 2.4mg weekly; Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes at doses up to 2mg, and prescribing it for weight loss is off-label use. The efficacy data supporting semaglutide as a weight loss therapy — including the STEP 1 trial's finding of 14.9% mean body weight reduction over 68 weeks — was generated at the Wegovy dose. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Wegovy the same drug as Ozempic?

Both Wegovy and Ozempic contain semaglutide, but they are different products with different FDA approvals, different maximum doses, and different intended patient populations. Wegovy is approved for chronic weight management at 2.4mg weekly; Ozempic is approved for type 2 diabetes at doses up to 2mg weekly.

Is Ozempic FDA-approved for weight loss?

No. Ozempic received FDA approval in December 2017 for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular risk reduction in diabetic patients — weight loss is an established off-label use. Wegovy, by contrast, is specifically FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with a comorbidity.

What were the results of the STEP 1 trial for Wegovy?

The STEP 1 trial, published by Wilding et al. in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2021, showed that semaglutide 2.4mg produced an average 14.9% reduction in body weight over 68 weeks in adults with obesity or overweight without type 2 diabetes. Approximately 86% of participants lost at least 5% of their body weight on the drug.

What BMI do I need to qualify for Wegovy?

FDA labeling for Wegovy covers adults with a BMI of 30 or greater (obesity), or adults with a BMI of 27 or greater who have at least one weight-related comorbidity — such as hypertension, high cholesterol, or type 2 diabetes. A licensed provider can confirm whether you meet these criteria during an initial consultation.

Why do some providers prescribe Ozempic instead of Wegovy for weight loss?

In some cases, off-label prescribing of Ozempic for weight loss reflects insurance dynamics — Ozempic may be covered for patients with type 2 diabetes while Wegovy coverage depends on whether a plan includes weight management benefits. In other cases, it reflects prescriber familiarity with the product. Patients should ask their provider to explain the rationale for whichever product is prescribed.

How does Wegovy compare to tirzepatide (Zepbound) for weight loss?

Both are approved for chronic weight management, but the clinical trial data shows tirzepatide producing greater average weight loss. The SURMOUNT 1 trial (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2022) showed an average 22.5% body weight reduction with tirzepatide 15mg over 72 weeks, compared to 14.9% for semaglutide 2.4mg in STEP 1. However, individual response varies, and the right medication depends on a patient's full clinical picture.

Does Wegovy have cardiovascular benefits beyond weight loss?

Yes. The SELECT trial demonstrated that semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events — including heart attack and stroke — by 20% in overweight and obese adults who did not have type 2 diabetes. This was a significant finding because it positioned semaglutide not merely as a weight loss drug but as a cardiovascular risk reduction therapy in this population.
