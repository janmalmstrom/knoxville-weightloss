Why East Tennessee adults — including Blount County residents — are qualified candidates

Tennessee's adult obesity rate is approximately 36% — one of the highest in the country. That figure reflects a real metabolic disease burden across Knox County, Blount County, and the communities between them. Obesity is a chronic disease driven by identifiable biology: dysregulation of appetite-signaling hormones, including GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1), sustains hunger at body weights the brain actively defends as normal. Repeated diet attempts fail not from insufficient effort, but because they do not address this underlying physiology.

GLP-1 receptor agonists — semaglutide and tirzepatide — act directly on those mechanisms. They activate GLP-1 receptors in the hypothalamus to reduce appetite signaling, slow gastric emptying, and improve satiety. The clinical outcomes are well-documented:

  • STEP 1 trial (semaglutide 2.4mg): Average 14.9% body weight loss over 68 weeks, versus 2.4% with placebo (Wilding et al., 2021, NEJM)
  • SURMOUNT 1 trial (tirzepatide 15mg): Average 22.5% body weight loss over 72 weeks, versus 2.4% with placebo (Jastreboff et al., 2022, NEJM)
  • SELECT trial (semaglutide 2.4mg): 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease, without diabetes — directly relevant to patients managing weight alongside cardiovascular risk (Lincoff et al., 2023, NEJM)

No non-surgical intervention had previously achieved outcomes at this scale in large randomized controlled trials. For adults in Maryville who have tried structured diet programs, exercise regimens, or commercial weight loss products without durable results, these medications address the biology those approaches cannot reach.

GLP-1 eligibility criteria — what Maryville and Blount County patients need to know

  • BMI ≥ 30: Qualifies for Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg) or Zepbound (tirzepatide) under FDA-approved indications
  • BMI ≥ 27 with a weight-related comorbidity: Hypertension, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, or obstructive sleep apnea each qualify
  • No personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN2 syndrome: These are contraindications for all GLP-1 receptor agonists
  • Not pregnant: GLP-1 therapy is not indicated during pregnancy
  • Baseline labs required: Thyroid panel, comprehensive metabolic panel, HbA1c — completed before initiating medication

Which medications are available — and what "FDA-approved" actually means for each

This distinction matters, and any credible provider will explain it rather than gloss over it.

Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4mg weekly injection) received FDA approval in June 2021 specifically for chronic weight management in adults with BMI ≥30, or BMI ≥27 with at least one weight-related condition. This approval was based on the STEP clinical trial program. Wegovy is the appropriate branded semaglutide for patients pursuing weight loss as the primary treatment goal.

Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg) is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes management. Weight loss at Ozempic doses is a well-documented clinical effect, but Ozempic is not FDA-approved for weight management independent of diabetes. Providers prescribing it for weight loss in non-diabetic patients are doing so off-label — which is legal and common in clinical practice, but patients deserve to understand the distinction.

Zepbound (tirzepatide 2.5–15mg weekly injection) received FDA approval in November 2023 for chronic weight management. Tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist — it activates two distinct receptor pathways rather than one, which likely contributes to the stronger average weight loss seen in the SURMOUNT trials compared with semaglutide in the STEP trials, though no head-to-head randomized trial has directly compared the two agents.

Mounjaro (tirzepatide 2.5–15mg) is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes. As with Ozempic, prescribing Mounjaro for weight loss in non-diabetic patients is an off-label use.

Compounded semaglutide — widely available through telehealth services — is not FDA-approved. It was permitted under drug shortage provisions while branded semaglutide remained unavailable. As the FDA resolves those shortages, it has been restricting compounding pharmacy production. The clinical efficacy data supporting semaglutide were generated using Novo Nordisk's pharmaceutical-grade formulation, not compounded versions. Patients should understand this before selecting a provider based primarily on compounded medication pricing.

⚠️ Important: If a provider advertises "FDA-approved Ozempic for weight loss" or "FDA-approved Mounjaro for weight loss," that language is inaccurate. Ozempic and Mounjaro carry FDA approval for type 2 diabetes only. Wegovy and Zepbound are the FDA-approved weight management formulations. A physician-supervised program should be transparent about this distinction.

What physician supervision actually includes — and why it matters for Maryville patients

The difference between a telehealth prescription service and a physician-supervised GLP-1 program is not the medication — it is the clinical infrastructure around it. GLP-1 therapy is highly effective, but appropriate management is required to achieve optimal outcomes and reduce the side effects that lead patients to discontinue prematurely.

The providers accessible through Knoxville Weight Loss Clinic include the following in supervised care:

  • Baseline lab work: Thyroid function, comprehensive metabolic panel, HbA1c — to establish a starting point and screen for contraindications
  • Structured dose titration: Both semaglutide and tirzepatide are initiated at low doses and increased gradually — typically over 16–20 weeks — to minimize gastrointestinal side effects including nausea, vomiting, and constipation
  • Monthly monitoring: Weight, blood pressure, and symptom review at each titration step
  • Side effect management: Dietary modifications, timing adjustments, and dose holds when clinically appropriate — managed by a physician, not an automated platform
  • Long-term planning: GLP-1 therapy is a long-term intervention. A supervised program addresses maintenance dosing strategies and what clinical management looks like at goal weight
