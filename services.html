Semaglutide (Wegovy) for Weight Loss

Wegovy is semaglutide 2.4mg — the FDA-approved formulation for chronic weight management. It is not the same as Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5–2mg, FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes). The distinction matters: Wegovy is specifically dosed and indicated for weight loss in patients with BMI 30 or higher, or BMI 27 or higher with a weight-related health condition such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes, or high cholesterol.

The STEP 1 clinical trial (published in NEJM, 2021) showed semaglutide 2.4mg produced an average 14.9% reduction in body weight over 68 weeks versus 2.4% for placebo. That is roughly 30–35 lbs on an average 220 lb patient. No previously available non-surgical intervention has produced results at this scale consistently.

Semaglutide works by mimicking the GLP-1 hormone — signaling the hypothalamus to reduce hunger, slowing gastric emptying so you feel full longer, and improving insulin sensitivity. It is a weekly subcutaneous injection, self-administered after physician training.

Who Qualifies

  • BMI ≥30 (obesity)
  • BMI ≥27 with hypertension, T2D, or high cholesterol
  • No personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
  • No personal or family history of MEN2
  • Not pregnant

Titration Schedule

  • Weeks 1–4: 0.25mg weekly
  • Weeks 5–8: 0.5mg weekly
  • Weeks 9–12: 1.0mg weekly
  • Weeks 13–16: 1.7mg weekly
  • Week 17+: 2.4mg weekly (maintenance)

Common Side Effects

  • Nausea (most common, especially early)
  • Vomiting
  • Constipation or diarrhea
  • Fatigue (typically early in titration)
  • Most GI side effects diminish after 4–8 weeks

What We Include

  • Physician consultation and eligibility evaluation
  • Baseline lab work (thyroid, metabolic, HbA1c)
  • Titration monitoring (monthly check-ins)
  • Injection training
  • Side effect management

Tirzepatide (Zepbound) for Weight Loss

Zepbound is tirzepatide 2.5–15mg — the FDA-approved formulation for chronic weight management. (Mounjaro is tirzepatide FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; weight loss is off-label for Mounjaro specifically.) Zepbound is the first dual GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonist approved for weight management, and its efficacy data is currently the strongest available for any non-surgical intervention.

The SURMOUNT 1 trial showed tirzepatide at 15mg produced an average 22.5% reduction in body weight over 72 weeks — roughly 50 lbs on a 220 lb patient. SURMOUNT 2 confirmed similar results in patients with type 2 diabetes. The dual mechanism (both GLP-1 and GIP receptor activation) appears to produce greater appetite suppression and metabolic effects than GLP-1 alone.

Who Qualifies

  • BMI ≥30 (obesity)
  • BMI ≥27 with hypertension, T2D, or high cholesterol
  • No personal or family history of MTC or MEN2
  • Not pregnant
  • May be appropriate after inadequate semaglutide response

Titration Schedule

  • Weeks 1–4: 2.5mg weekly
  • Weeks 5–8: 5mg weekly
  • Weeks 9–12: 7.5mg weekly (optional maintenance)
  • Weeks 13–16: 10mg weekly
  • Week 17+: 12.5mg or 15mg (maintenance)

Semaglutide vs Tirzepatide

  • Both: weekly injection, GLP-1 mechanism
  • Tirzepatide: adds GIP agonism (dual mechanism)
  • Tirzepatide: stronger average efficacy in trials
  • Semaglutide: longer on market, more real-world data
  • Both: require physician supervision and monitoring

What We Include

  • Physician consultation and eligibility evaluation
  • Baseline lab work (thyroid, metabolic, HbA1c)
  • Titration monitoring (monthly check-ins)
  • Injection training
  • Side effect management
Ozempic & Mounjaro — Off-Label Weight Loss

GLP-1 Therapy for Patients with Type 2 Diabetes

Patients with type 2 diabetes often ask about using Ozempic (semaglutide) or Mounjaro (tirzepatide) for weight loss. To be precise: Ozempic is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, not for weight management — weight loss is an established and well-documented off-label effect. The same applies to Mounjaro. This does not mean these medications are inappropriate for weight loss in diabetic patients — off-label prescribing is standard medical practice and these drugs are among the most prescribed medications in the US. It means the prescribing physician is responsible for evaluating the risk/benefit for each patient.

For patients with T2D, GLP-1 therapy often serves a dual purpose: improving glycemic control and producing significant weight loss. The weight loss itself improves insulin sensitivity, often allowing medication reduction over time. We evaluate each patient's full medical picture and prescribe the medication that best fits their situation.

Coverage & Cost

Insurance Coverage and Pricing

Insurance coverage for GLP-1 weight loss medications remains inconsistent and is one of the most common questions we receive. Here is an honest summary of the current landscape.

When Insurance Covers Wegovy / Zepbound

  • Some commercial plans cover with prior authorization
  • Typically requires BMI ≥30 or ≥27 with documented comorbidity
  • Often requires documented failure of behavioral intervention
  • Medicare Part D: covered as of 2026 for cardiovascular risk patients
  • Medicaid TennCare: coverage varies — call to verify

Self-Pay Cost Estimates

  • Wegovy: $1,300–$1,600/month retail (manufacturer savings cards may apply)
  • Zepbound: $1,000–$1,400/month retail (Eli Lilly savings program)
  • Physician consultation: call for current pricing
  • Lab work: typically billed to insurance separately
  • Monthly monitoring: call for current pricing

Manufacturer Savings Programs

  • Novo Nordisk WeGo Care (Wegovy): eligible patients may pay $0–$25/month
  • Eli Lilly savings card (Zepbound): eligible patients may pay $550/month
  • Eligibility requires commercial insurance (not Medicare/Medicaid)
  • We assist with prior authorization paperwork

Compounded Semaglutide — A Note

  • Compounded semaglutide is NOT FDA-approved
  • Was permitted under shortage provisions; FDA restricting compounding as shortages resolve
  • Purity and concentration vary between compounding pharmacies
  • We prescribe FDA-approved brand medications only
  • We explain this distinction clearly before any prescribing decision
