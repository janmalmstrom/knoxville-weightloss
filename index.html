The Biology

Obesity Is a Metabolic Disease — GLP-1 Therapy Addresses the Biology

If repeated diet and exercise attempts haven't produced lasting weight loss, that is not a willpower failure. Obesity involves chronic changes in appetite hormones, metabolic rate, and the brain's hunger signals. GLP-1 receptor agonists target those mechanisms directly — reducing appetite signals in the hypothalamus and slowing gastric emptying so you feel full on less food.

The STEP 1 trial showed semaglutide produced an average 15% reduction in body weight over 68 weeks — significantly more than any previously available non-surgical intervention. The SURMOUNT 1 trial showed tirzepatide averaging 20–22%. Tennessee has one of the highest obesity rates in the US (approximately 36%). Physician-supervised GLP-1 therapy is now the standard of care for obesity medicine.

Semaglutide (Wegovy)

FDA-approved for chronic weight management (June 2021). Eligible for BMI 30 or higher, or BMI 27 or higher with a weight-related condition. 15% average body weight loss in STEP trials over 68 weeks.

Tirzepatide (Zepbound)

FDA-approved for chronic weight management (November 2023). Dual GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonist. 20–22% average body weight loss in SURMOUNT trials — the strongest efficacy data currently available.

Type 2 Diabetes + Weight

Patients with T2D often ask about Ozempic or Mounjaro for weight loss. These are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; weight loss is a well-established off-label benefit. We evaluate each patient individually.

Cardiovascular Risk Reduction

The SELECT trial showed semaglutide reduced major cardiovascular events by 20% in overweight and obese patients without diabetes. Weight loss is now an evidence-based cardiovascular intervention.

Post-Menopausal Weight Gain

Hormonal shifts after menopause change fat distribution and metabolic rate. GLP-1 therapy addresses the underlying biology — not just calorie restriction — which is why it works when diet programs don't.

What to Expect

The Treatment Process

GLP-1 therapy is not a prescription and a wish. Effective treatment requires proper evaluation, dose titration, and monitoring. Here is how we approach it.

1

Physician Consultation

A full medical history review. We evaluate BMI, current health conditions, medications, and treatment history to determine eligibility and identify the right medication for you.

2

Baseline Lab Work

Thyroid panel, metabolic panel, and HbA1c at minimum. Some patients also need lipid panel and kidney function. Lab work is included in your initial evaluation.

3

Starting Dose + Titration Schedule

Both semaglutide and tirzepatide are started at low doses and increased slowly — typically monthly — to reduce nausea and GI side effects. Most patients reach their maintenance dose by week 16–20.

4

Monthly Monitoring

Check-ins to review weight, side effects, and dose. If nausea or other GI symptoms are significant, we adjust. Most side effects occur at lower doses and diminish as the body adapts.

5

Ongoing Care & Maintenance

Weight loss maintenance requires continued therapy for most patients. The biology of weight regain after stopping medication is well-documented. We plan for long-term management, not just a short course.
